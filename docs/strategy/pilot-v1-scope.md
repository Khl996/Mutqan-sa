# Mutqan Controlled Pilot v1 Scope

## 1. Executive Summary

Mutqan Controlled Pilot v1 is a focused, time-boxed implementation of Mutqan for early customers who need a practical operations, maintenance, and asset management foundation.

Mutqan is not only a work order system. It is designed to become the living operational memory of a facility and a decision-support companion for maintenance, facility, and asset managers. The long-term product direction includes intelligent operations, stronger integrations, and richer operational insight. Pilot v1 does not attempt to deliver that full vision. It proves the foundation required for that vision to become credible.

The golden rule for Pilot v1 is that every included feature must support at least one of the following:

1. Document what happens.
2. Connect what happens to its operational context.
3. Turn what happens into a decision.

Pilot v1 should be sold and delivered as a controlled operational foundation pilot, not as an open-ended custom implementation.

## 2. What Pilot v1 Is

Pilot v1 is a controlled, realistic introduction of Mutqan into one customer environment. It is intended to validate whether Mutqan can help the customer document maintenance activity, connect work to assets and locations, and use basic operational evidence to improve decisions.

Pilot v1 includes:

- A limited operational setup for one customer organization.
- A focused set of users, locations, assets, work orders, preventive maintenance activities, inventory records, and reports.
- A clear start date, pilot period, review process, and conversion path.
- A shared agreement on what success means before the pilot begins.
- A product-led implementation using configuration and guided setup, not bespoke software delivery.

Pilot v1 is successful when the customer can see Mutqan becoming the operational record for selected facilities, assets, and maintenance workflows.

## 3. What Pilot v1 Is Not

Pilot v1 is not the full long-term Mutqan platform vision.

It is not:

- A full enterprise transformation project.
- A replacement for every existing system on day one.
- A custom software development engagement.
- A deep integration project.
- A full IoT, BMS, BIM, or automation rollout.
- A native mobile app delivery commitment.
- An AI assistant deployment.
- A guarantee that every internal process will be modeled exactly as-is.
- A production-critical emergency response system unless separately agreed in writing.

Mutqan is designed to evolve toward intelligent operations and integrations, but Pilot v1 focuses on the operational foundation: structured records, connected context, disciplined workflows, and useful reporting.

## 4. Target Early Customers

Pilot v1 is best suited for customers who have real operational complexity but can accept a controlled pilot boundary.

Ideal early customers include:

- Facility management companies running maintenance across one or more buildings.
- Residential or commercial compounds with active maintenance teams.
- Multi-site maintenance projects that need consistent operational records across locations.
- Hospitals, clinics, schools, offices, warehouses, or other regulated and specialized environments where the pilot scope can remain controlled.
- Asset-heavy organizations that currently rely on spreadsheets, paper, WhatsApp, email, or disconnected tools.
- Teams with a clear maintenance manager, facility manager, or operations owner who can act as the pilot champion.
- Customers willing to start with a selected site, asset group, or team before scaling.

Poor-fit early customers include:

- Customers requiring enterprise integrations before any operational value can be proven.
- Customers who cannot assign a pilot owner.
- Customers expecting unlimited customization during the pilot.
- Customers who want to evaluate Mutqan without using real workflows, users, and operational data.

## 5. Included Modules and Workflows

Pilot v1 includes the minimum set of modules needed to prove Mutqan's operational foundation.

Mutqan treats locations as a flexible operational hierarchy, not only as buildings and floors. A pilot structure may represent facility -> building -> floor -> room, project -> site -> unit, or area -> asset group.

| Area | Included Scope | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organization setup | Customer tenant, core settings, selected roles, selected users | Establish a controlled operating environment |
| Facilities and locations | One selected facility or a limited facility hierarchy | Connect work to where it happens |
| Assets | Selected asset register with essential metadata, location, criticality, and QR-ready identity where available | Connect work to the equipment or object affected |
| Work requests | Basic request intake and triage workflow | Document issues and operational demand |
| Corrective work orders | Create, assign, update, complete, and review work orders | Document maintenance execution |
| Preventive maintenance | Limited preventive maintenance schedules, checklists, and generated work orders | Prove planned work discipline |
| Teams and assignments | Basic team structure, responsible users, and technician assignment | Connect work to ownership |
| Status and audit trail | Status movement, timestamps, notes, and completion evidence | Preserve operational memory |
| Proof of Work | Basic completion evidence for selected work orders, including timestamps, responsible users, notes, attachments/photos where applicable, and approval history | Make completed work traceable and reviewable |
| Attachments and notes | Photos, notes, and supporting evidence within agreed limits | Capture practical field context |
| Basic inventory | Selected spare parts, stock levels, low-stock visibility, and consumption against work where available | Connect maintenance activity to material usage |
| Reports and KPIs | Operational dashboards and reports that identify basic operational signals, not only activity counts, including overdue work, repeated demand by location or asset, PM completion gaps, and inventory or workload risks where data exists | Turn activity into management decisions |

The pilot should demonstrate a connected chain such as:

`Location -> Asset -> Work Request -> Work Order -> Assignment -> Execution Evidence -> Completion -> Report`

Where preventive maintenance is included, the pilot should also demonstrate:

`Asset -> PM Schedule -> Generated Work Order -> Checklist Execution -> Completion -> PM Compliance`

## 6. Explicitly Excluded Items

The following items are excluded from Pilot v1 unless separately scoped, priced, and approved:

- IoT device connectivity.
- BMS integration.
- BIM model integration or BIM viewer workflows.
- AI assistant, chatbot, predictive AI, or automated recommendation engine.
- Native iOS or Android application.
- Offline-first mobile execution.
- Deep integrations with ERP, finance, HR, procurement, CAFM, CMMS, SSO, access control, or vendor systems.
- Custom APIs for a specific customer.
- Custom database schema changes for a specific customer.
- Enterprise data migration from legacy systems beyond agreed pilot import templates.
- Full historical data cleanup.
- Full compliance, accreditation, or regulatory implementation.
- Advanced contract lifecycle management.
- Advanced procurement workflows.
- Advanced inventory valuation, purchasing, or warehouse management.
- Complex approval chains beyond the supported pilot workflow.
- Per-customer UI redesign.
- Per-customer code forks.
- 24/7 support, emergency dispatch, or managed maintenance operations.

Excluded items may be considered for the product roadmap, annual subscription expansion, or a separately contracted professional services engagement.

## 7. Pilot Duration and Phases

The recommended Pilot v1 duration is 8 to 12 weeks.

| Phase | Duration | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualification | 1 week | Confirm fit, pilot owner, selected scope, success criteria, and go/no-go conditions |
| Setup | 1 to 2 weeks | Configure tenant, users, locations, selected assets, workflows, and initial data |
| Enablement | 1 week | Train pilot users and confirm the first operational workflows |
| Live pilot | 4 to 6 weeks | Run real work requests, work orders, PM activities, inventory usage, and reporting |
| Review and conversion | 1 to 2 weeks | Review success criteria, risks, adoption, and annual subscription decision |

The pilot may be shorter only if the customer already has clean data, a ready champion, and a narrow operational scope.

## 8. Minimum Viable Setup Requirements

Pilot v1 should not begin until the following minimum setup requirements are met:

- One named executive or management sponsor.
- One named pilot owner responsible for daily coordination.
- One selected facility, area, or asset group.
- A defined pilot user list with roles.
- A starter asset list with essential fields.
- A starter location structure.
- At least one real corrective maintenance workflow.
- At least one preventive maintenance workflow if PM is included in the pilot.
- Agreement on which reports and KPIs will be reviewed.
- Agreement on what data will be imported manually or through templates.
- Agreement that excluded items will not block pilot success.

Minimum recommended pilot data:

- 1 customer organization.
- 1 selected facility or controlled location hierarchy.
- 20 to 100 selected assets, depending on customer readiness.
- 5 to 20 pilot users.
- 3 to 10 preventive maintenance schedules where PM is in scope.
- 20 or more real work orders during the live pilot period.
- A small set of inventory items if inventory is in scope.

## 9. Success Criteria

Pilot success should be measured against operational proof, not feature quantity.

Recommended success criteria:

- The customer completes real work orders in Mutqan during the pilot period.
- Selected assets and locations are connected to actual work activity.
- Technicians or responsible users update work status, notes, and completion evidence.
- Managers can see open, overdue, completed, and preventive work activity without relying only on spreadsheets or chat.
- Preventive maintenance schedules produce useful work records where PM is in scope.
- Inventory consumption or low-stock visibility is demonstrated where inventory is in scope.
- The customer can identify at least three operational decisions improved by Mutqan data.
- The customer agrees that Mutqan can become the operational memory for the selected scope.
- The customer identifies a clear next scope for annual subscription expansion.

Examples of decisions Pilot v1 should support:

- Which work is overdue and needs escalation.
- Which assets or locations generate repeated maintenance demand.
- Which preventive tasks are being completed on time.
- Which spare parts are at risk of shortage.
- Which teams or users carry the active workload.

## 10. Customer Responsibilities

The customer is responsible for making the pilot real enough to be meaningful.

Customer responsibilities include:

- Assigning a sponsor and pilot owner.
- Providing accurate starter data for users, locations, assets, PM schedules, and inventory where applicable.
- Selecting the pilot scope and avoiding uncontrolled expansion during the pilot.
- Ensuring pilot users attend onboarding sessions.
- Using Mutqan for agreed workflows during the live pilot period.
- During the live pilot, chat messages, spreadsheets, or informal channels should not be treated as the primary source of truth for agreed pilot workflows. They may support communication, but operational records should be captured in Mutqan.
- Providing timely feedback on workflow fit, data quality, and reporting usefulness.
- Avoiding sensitive, regulated, or confidential data unless explicitly agreed.
- Making a timely go/no-go and conversion decision at the end of the pilot.

## 11. Mutqan Responsibilities

Mutqan is responsible for delivering the agreed pilot setup and guiding the customer toward operational proof.

Mutqan responsibilities include:

- Confirming pilot fit before kickoff.
- Setting up the pilot tenant and agreed configuration.
- Providing setup templates or guidance for required data.
- Supporting initial import or manual entry of agreed pilot data.
- Training agreed pilot users.
- Supporting the customer during the live pilot within agreed boundaries.
- Reviewing adoption, workflow use, and reported issues.
- Providing a pilot review summary and annual subscription recommendation.
- Maintaining the product roadmap distinction between pilot scope and future platform vision.

## 12. Support Boundaries

Pilot v1 support is intended to help the customer adopt the agreed workflows, not to operate the customer's maintenance function.

Included support:

- Setup guidance.
- User onboarding.
- Workflow clarification.
- Bug triage.
- Reasonable configuration support.
- Pilot review meetings.
- Guidance on reports and operational interpretation.

Excluded support:

- 24/7 support unless separately contracted.
- Emergency dispatch or operational command services.
- Customer-side data cleansing beyond agreed templates.
- Customer-side process redesign unrelated to Mutqan use.
- Support for excluded integrations or custom features.
- On-site support unless separately agreed.
- Support for unsupported browsers, devices, or network environments.

Support channels, response expectations, and escalation contacts should be confirmed in the pilot agreement.

## 13. Customization Boundaries

Pilot v1 customization should use supported configuration only.

Pilot v1 customization is configuration-led. Mutqan should adapt through supported settings, labels, roles, workflows, locations, and templates where available, not through customer-specific code forks.

Allowed configuration may include:

- Roles and user access within supported permission models.
- Selected workflow statuses where supported.
- Basic naming, labels, or operational categories where supported.
- Facility, location, asset, and team structures.
- PM schedules and checklist content.
- Report filters or views where supported.

Not allowed in Pilot v1:

- Custom product modules.
- Customer-specific code changes.
- Customer-specific database schema changes.
- Custom integration development.
- Custom UI redesign.
- Unlimited approval workflow modeling.
- Per-customer business logic forks.

Requests outside supported configuration should be recorded as one of:

- Product roadmap candidate.
- Annual subscription expansion item.
- Separately priced professional services item.
- Out-of-scope request.

## 14. Data and Security Assumptions

Pilot v1 assumes a standard SaaS deployment model with tenant-level separation and role-based access.

Data and security assumptions:

- Customer data remains associated with the customer's tenant.
- Users access the system through assigned roles and permissions.
- The pilot should use only operational data required for the agreed workflows.
- Sensitive personal, patient, financial, or regulated data should not be entered unless explicitly reviewed and approved.
- Data import is limited to agreed templates and practical pilot needs.
- Historical data migration is not required for pilot success.
- Attachments should be limited to operational evidence relevant to work orders and assets.
- Customer administrators are responsible for confirming user access and removing users who should no longer participate.
- Standard platform security controls apply; customer-specific compliance commitments require separate review.

Pilot v1 should prove operational value without requiring the customer to expose unnecessary sensitive data.

## 15. Go/No-Go Criteria

Pilot v1 should proceed only when the following go criteria are met:

- The customer matches the target early customer profile.
- The customer accepts the controlled pilot scope.
- A sponsor and pilot owner are assigned.
- Minimum setup data is available or can be prepared quickly.
- The customer accepts the explicit exclusions.
- Success criteria are agreed before kickoff.
- The pilot can be completed within the recommended duration.
- No excluded item is treated as a blocker to pilot success.

Pilot v1 should not proceed, or should be paused, if:

- The customer requires IoT, BMS, BIM, AI assistant, native mobile app, or deep integrations as a pilot dependency.
- The customer cannot provide a pilot owner.
- The customer cannot provide enough real workflow activity.
- The customer expects unlimited customization.
- The customer's security or compliance requirements exceed the standard pilot assumptions.
- The customer is unwilling to make a conversion decision after the pilot review.

## 16. Conversion Criteria From Pilot To Annual Subscription

Conversion from Pilot v1 to an annual subscription should be recommended when:

- The customer used Mutqan for real operational workflows during the pilot.
- The agreed success criteria were met or materially validated.
- Managers see value in connected work, asset, location, and reporting records.
- The customer identifies a clear expanded scope for the next subscription period.
- Required changes for continued use are mostly configuration, training, or roadmap-aligned improvements.
- Commercial terms, user counts, modules, support level, and implementation expectations are agreed.

Conversion should be delayed or rejected when:

- The customer did not use the system for real workflows.
- Success depends primarily on excluded items.
- The customer requires enterprise customization before adopting the platform.
- Data quality or ownership issues remain unresolved.
- There is no accountable operational owner for the annual subscription.

## 17. Risks And Mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scope creep | Pilot becomes too broad to prove value | Confirm exclusions and maintain a change log for future phases |
| Weak customer ownership | Low adoption and unclear feedback | Require sponsor and pilot owner before kickoff |
| Poor starter data | Setup delays and unreliable reports | Use minimum data templates and limit initial scope |
| Pilot treated as a demo only | No evidence of operational value | Require real work orders and real users during the live phase |
| Integration dependency | Pilot blocked by systems outside Mutqan | Exclude deep integrations and use manual or template-based setup |
| Over-customization pressure | Product focus is diluted | Allow configuration only and record requests for later review |
| Reporting expectations exceed data maturity | Customer distrusts early reports | Explain that reports reflect entered data quality and pilot scope |
| User resistance | Work continues outside Mutqan | Train a focused user group and align pilot workflows with daily work |
| Security concerns | Customer hesitates to use real data | Limit data to necessary operational records and avoid sensitive data |
| Conversion ambiguity | Pilot ends without a decision | Define conversion criteria and review date before kickoff |

## 18. Final Decision Statement

Mutqan Controlled Pilot v1 is approved as a focused operational foundation pilot.

Its purpose is to prove that Mutqan can document what happens, connect work to operational context, and turn operational records into management decisions for a controlled customer scope.

Pilot v1 must not promise IoT, BMS, BIM, AI assistant capabilities, native mobile apps, deep integrations, or enterprise customization as included deliverables. These remain future product directions, roadmap candidates, or separately scoped engagements.

The official Pilot v1 offer should be sold, implemented, supported, and reviewed according to this scope. Any request outside this scope requires explicit approval before it is presented to a customer as committed work.
