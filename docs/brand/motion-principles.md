# Mutqan Motion Principles

## Purpose
This document defines how motion should be used across Mutqan product, marketing, sales, and educational materials.

Motion in Mutqan is not entertainment. Motion should explain operational flow, show change over time, and guide attention without distracting from the work.

## Motion philosophy
Mutqan motion must be calm, precise, and purposeful.

Motion should help explain:
- How a request becomes a work order.
- How a work order connects to an asset.
- How a completed job enters asset history.
- How operational data becomes a report.
- How a report becomes a decision signal.

If motion does not explain, guide, or confirm something, it should not be used.

## Core motion principles

### 1. Motion explains workflow
The primary role of motion is to make operational flow understandable.

Good uses:
- A line connecting request to location, asset, and report.
- A work order card transforming into an asset history event.
- A report card revealing a decision signal.
- A timeline building event by event.

Weak uses:
- Random floating shapes.
- Continuous decorative movement.
- Overly dramatic transitions.
- Motion that slows task completion.

### 2. Product motion must be quieter than marketing motion
Product UI is a daily workspace. Motion inside the product must be minimal and functional.

Marketing pages and videos can use richer motion to explain ideas, but they should still feel calm and credible.

### 3. Motion should direct attention
Motion should help the user notice:
- A status change.
- A completed action.
- A new risk signal.
- A required approval.
- A loading or progress state.

Motion should not compete with the primary task.

### 4. Motion must respect operational seriousness
Mutqan is used in maintenance and facility operations. The motion language must not feel playful, childish, or game-like.

Motion should feel:
- Smooth.
- Measured.
- Controlled.
- Professional.
- Useful.

## Approved motion patterns

### 1. Line drawing
Use for connected workflow explanations.

Examples:
- Request connects to asset.
- Asset connects to report.
- Location connects to risk signal.

### 2. Timeline build
Use to show operational memory forming over time.

Examples:
- Work order created.
- Technician assigned.
- Work started.
- Evidence added.
- Supervisor approved.
- Asset record updated.

### 3. Card transform
Use to show one operational object becoming another record.

Examples:
- Request becomes work order.
- Completed work becomes proof card.
- Work order event becomes asset history.

### 4. Reveal
Use to introduce layers of information progressively.

Examples:
- Show status first, then owner, then asset, then report signal.
- Reveal data interpretation after chart values.

### 5. Pulse
Use sparingly for attention signals.

Examples:
- Critical asset.
- Overdue work.
- Facility Pulse.
- Review-needed insight.

Pulse must be subtle and should not run forever unless the state is actively critical and the context justifies it.

### 6. Progress motion
Use for loading, generation, export, or long-running actions.

Examples:
- Generating report.
- Exporting PDF.
- Loading tenant data.
- Syncing updates.

## Product motion rules

### Allowed in product
- Loading indicators.
- Small hover/focus transitions.
- Subtle status feedback.
- Accordion open/close.
- Timeline reveal.
- Toast entrance/exit.
- Subtle risk pulse.

### Avoid in product
- Decorative loops.
- Heavy page transitions.
- Overly animated dashboards.
- Long motion sequences before users can act.
- Motion that distracts technicians in field use.

## Marketing motion rules

Marketing motion can be more expressive, but must remain operational.

Allowed:
- Operating Graph animation.
- Before/After transformation.
- Asset Passport reveal.
- Proof of Work card build.
- Decision Brief reveal.
- Facility Pulse concept animation.

Avoid:
- Futuristic effects that imply unbuilt AI/IoT/BIM features.
- Excessive 3D motion.
- Generic SaaS animations.
- Motion templates that do not feel like Mutqan.

## Motion timing principles

General guidance:
- Micro-interactions: fast and subtle.
- Section reveals: calm and readable.
- Explainer animations: paced for understanding.
- Loading states: clear and reassuring.

Motion should never make the interface feel slower.

## Accessibility and usability

Motion should respect users who prefer reduced motion.

Future implementation should consider:
- Reduced motion support.
- Avoiding flashing effects.
- Avoiding rapid repeated animation.
- Ensuring motion is not the only way information is communicated.

## Motion governance checklist

Before approving motion, ask:

1. What does this motion explain?
2. Does it guide attention to a useful action or signal?
3. Is it calm and professional?
4. Does it avoid implying unbuilt features?
5. Does it slow down user work?
6. Would it still make sense in Arabic and English contexts?
7. Is it repeatable as part of a system?

## Employee onboarding summary

Motion in Mutqan should make operations easier to understand. It should show flow, memory, proof, and decision signals. It must never become decoration that distracts from maintenance work or overpromises product capabilities.
